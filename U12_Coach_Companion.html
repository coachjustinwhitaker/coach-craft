Coach Craft  |  Player Development Methodology

U12 Coach Companion Me & the Ball  ·  Me & My Teammates  ·  Me & My Team Defending  ·  Me & the Game

Foundation Phase III  ·  9v9  ·  2 Practices Per Week  ·  Recreational
Session Length:Up to 90 minutes total  ·  Arrive 15 minutes early

The game is full now. Nine against nine means every player has a role with the ball and without it. The players in front of you are ready for that. They are ready to compete not just as individuals or pairs, but as a team that defends together and attacks together. Your job this season is to help them find that feeling of collective purpose. Enjoy watching them discover it.

What Each Focus Means at U12
Me & the Ball
Growth Phase
Ball skills, scanning, body shape. Players know these from U8 and U10 and are now deepening them under more pressure, on a bigger field, with more opponents.
Me & My Teammates
Growth Phase
Combination play, switching the point of attack, finding teammates in space. This goes deeper than U10 as the game gets more complex.
Me & My Team Defending
Reinforced
Compact team shape and team defending, reinforced and emphasized from U10. The core principles: one player pressures the ball, one covers behind them, the rest support to cut off space. US Soccer calls these pressure, cover, and support.
Me & the Game
Introduced
Both transition moments: what do we do the instant we lose the ball, and the instant we win it back.
US Soccer Alignment
Foundation Phase III  ·  9v9 Format

At U12, all four game moments become active coaching priorities: attacking, defending, and both transitions. “Me & the Ball” and “Me & My Teammates” are now in a growth phase. Players know these skills and are deepening them. “Me & My Team Defending” is reinforced and emphasized, and “Me & the Game” is introduced for the first time this season.

Who Is In Front of You
Understanding Your U12 Players

They Have Been Around the Game

Most of these players have been playing for several years. They are still developing their understanding that passing creates space and that defending takes real collective effort. They are ready to be coached on how their individual actions connect to what the team is doing.

How You Deliver Feedback Matters

Players at this age are more aware of how they look in front of their peers. When you have something to say to an individual, find something they did right first. Save whole-group feedback for moments of collective success, not individual correction.

Two Practices Build Habits

With two sessions a week, what you introduce on day one has time to settle before day two builds on it. Pay attention to what shows up in the second session without you asking for it. That carry-over is the sign the concept is taking hold.

Ask Them Why, Not Just What

Players at this age can answer deeper questions. “Why does switching the ball make the defender’s job harder?” is a question a U12 player can genuinely engage with. Use that.

What a Great U12 Session Looks Like
Immediate reaction to transition

When the ball turns over, do players react instantly? Or do they watch it happen? That first-second reaction is the whole “Me & the Game” introduction.

Compact defending shape

Are defenders close enough to support each other? The distance between first and second defender tells you everything about their shape.

Combination play happening naturally

Players who are passing and moving without being asked are showing that the habits from U10 are sticking. If it is not happening yet, go back to a simpler passing activity. The habit builds with repetition.

Players coaching each other

US Soccer notes this explicitly at U12: player-to-player coaching is a development goal, not just a nice thing to see. Step back when it happens.

Practice A concept in Practice B

If a concept introduced in the first session of the week appears in the second session without you asking for it. That is the whole point of two practices a week.

Everyone gets time in goal

Rotate the goalkeeper so every player experiences the position. If someone shows real enthusiasm for it, encourage that too. Positional identity starts forming at this age.

Teaching Points for This Age Group
What You Are Developing
ControllingProtectingDriving DribblingPassingShooting ScanningSupportingBody Shape CoveringMarking

Covering and Marking are the two defending perceptual skills players first met at U10. At U12 they move from individual actions into how the team defends. Covering means positioning close enough to the first defender to step in if they are beaten. Marking means tracking a specific opponent and denying them the ball in a good position. Together these two skills are how team defending starts to function rather than just individual reactions to the ball.

Before You Arrive
What Prepared Looks Like
Preparation at U12 matters more than at any previous age. You have four active focuses and a season arc that builds deliberately. Know which focus is the priority for this week before you arrive. With two practices per week, Practice A introduces the concept and Practice B deepens it. If the group needs more time on the same concept before moving forward, run the consolidation instead. Both are correct responses.
The Parents on the Sideline

Before the session starts, take 30 seconds to walk over. Something like:

“Today we are working on what happens when the ball changes hands, in the first second after we win it or lose it. The most helpful thing you can do is cheer the team moments: the player who sprints to press the second they lose the ball, the player who finds a teammate the instant they win it.”

At this age players are more aware of what the sideline is saying than they were at U8 or U10. Positive sideline energy is genuinely useful. Critical sideline energy genuinely disrupts.

Opening Play
5v5 or 6v6 – Free Small-Sided Game
Same setup every session. Smaller than the closing 9v9. More touches, cleaner decisions.

The Setup

Small goals or full goals depending on space. Teams of 5 or 6. No goalkeeper in the opening game. Add one in the closing 9v9. Quick restarts. Let them play the first 8 minutes without saying anything. On Practice B days, watch specifically for whether the concept from Practice A earlier in the week shows up.

Your Job

Watch the transition moments specifically. What happens in the first couple of seconds after the ball changes hands? That tells you which focus area to prioritize today. Come in with Activity A already chosen and let the opening game confirm your read.
One Question — Ask it after the opening game “When the ball changed hands today, when you won it or lost it, what was your first reaction?”
⚽ 1 ball per team🏈 Small or full goals🟠 Disc cones🟡 Pinnies — 2 colors
Practice
Activity Toolkit – Pick Two Per Session

Run Activity A for 15 minutes, then Activity B for 15 minutes. A brief transition or water break (“good work, new game”) resets attention and energy. Follow the season plan for which focus to prioritize each week.

The Pairing Rule Try to avoid pairing two new concepts in the same session. Activity A works best as something players already know well enough to run without much explanation. Activity B is where the new concept lives. If the group is flat after Activity A, shorten Activity B or swap it for something more physical.
ME & THE BALL & ME & MY TEAMMATES  —  Growth Phase: combination play, depth in shape, switching attack
Activity 01
Flying Numbers
Variable Pressure · Decision-Making · All Focuses
Setup
Mark space with cones, goals on both sides. Two teams along the touchline either side of you. Call a number combo and each side sends in that number to scrimmage. New players rotate in after every goal or ball out. Frame the transition explicitly: outgoing players sprint off, incoming players sprint on. No walking at any point.
What to Look For
The most adaptable game in the toolkit and the best anchor activity at U12. Works as pure anchor (high energy, familiar) or as a transition focus when you emphasize the urgency of sprinting in and out. If one player is dominating, play them 1v2.
“When you had more players than the other team, what did you do with that extra person?”
→ Pairs well with: Any activity in the toolkit. Works as an anchor for every week.
1 ball · 2 goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 02
Half and Half
Depth in Shape · Me & My Teammates
Setup
Rectangular grid with goals at each end and a midpoint line of cones. 3v3 or 4v4. Normal scrimmage, except a goal only counts if the attacking team has at least one player in both their attacking AND defending halves when the goal is scored. The player who stays back is providing depth and defensive security.
What to Look For
Forces players to spread across the length of the field, not just the width. The player who stays in the defending half might miss the goal celebration. Acknowledge them specifically. US Soccer introduces depth of shape explicitly at U12. This game teaches it without you naming it.
“The player who stayed back when your team scored: what were they protecting?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Barcelona
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones including midpoint line · Pinnies
Activity 03
3v3+1 Keep-Away
Possession · Transition · Me & My Teammates Deep
Setup
Square of cones for every 7 players. Two teams of 3 in different colors, one neutral player who plays with whoever has the ball. Attackers rack up passes. When defenders win it, the neutral player switches sides immediately. No delay. After a minute, switch roles.
What to Look For
The neutral player switching sides instantly is the whole point. That instant role switch seeds transition awareness naturally inside a possession game. Also look for players spreading their hips open to receive before the ball arrives. Works well as Activity B after Flying Numbers.
“When the ball turned over, how quickly did you read whose team you were joining or defending against?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Hotbox
1 ball per grid · 4 cones per square · 3 colors of pinnies
Activity 04
Barcelona
Patience · Pass Counting · Me & My Teammates Depth
Setup
Rectangular grid with goals at each end. 3v3 or 4v4, no goalkeeper. Score points equal to the number of passes completed before scoring: 8 passes before a goal = 8 points. A missed shot scores nothing. Play short rounds of 3–4 minutes.
What to Look For
Watch for players who switch the ball to the far side before going to goal, drawing defenders before finding the opening. Celebrate it when it happens by name. Best as a focus activity, not an anchor.
“Before you scored, was there a moment where you switched the ball to the other side? What did that open up?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or 3v3+1 Keep-Away
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
ME & MY TEAM DEFENDING  —  Reinforced: pressure, cover, support  ·  compact shape  ·  working together
Activity 05
2v2v2 Keep-Away
Compact Defending · First & Second Defender
Setup
Square of cones for every 6 players. Three pairs in different colors. Two pairs attack and keep the ball away from the defending pair. When defenders steal or force a mistake, they replace the attacking pair who last touched the ball. Immediately, no stoppages. The new defenders must get into position fast.
What to Look For
The defending pair must communicate constantly: who pressures the ball, who covers behind. Look for the pair who splits those jobs naturally. The team that defends compactly wins the ball most often.
“When you were the defending pair, how did you decide who would press the ball and who would cover?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Half Court Soccer
1 ball per grid · 4 cones per square · 3 colors of pinnies
Activity 06
Half Court Soccer
Compact Shape · Defending as a Team
Setup
Rectangular grid, wider than it is long. A check line at one end, the goal at the other. Both teams attack the same goal, like half-court basketball. If a team steals the ball, they must clear it by dribbling over the check line before they can attempt to score. Play 3v3 plus optional rotating goalkeeper.
What to Look For
The check line creates a natural defending moment. Defenders must get compact between the ball and the goal. When the attack clears the line and comes back, defenders must re-organize quickly. Watch for players who position between opponent and goal rather than just chasing the ball.
“When the other team was dribbling toward goal, where were you standing? Between the ball and the goal, or somewhere else?”
→ Pairs well with: 2v2v2 Keep-Away or Flying Numbers
1 ball · 1 goal · Disc cones including check line · Pinnies
Activity 07
Shrinking Field – Defending Focus
Compact Shape Under Pressure
Setup
Rectangular grid with small goals at each end. 3v3 or 4v4. Every minute, move each touchline in by 3 steps. Explicitly frame as a defending focus before starting: “today we are watching the defending team, not the attacking team.” Play 2–3 rounds.
What to Look For
As the field shrinks, the compact defending shape becomes more and more important. Watch for the defending team getting tighter: closer together, higher up, forcing play to one side. Are they reacting to the ball or anticipating where it is going? Any moment of anticipation is worth highlighting.
“As the field got smaller, did defending get easier or harder? What does that tell you about defending in tight spaces?”
→ Pairs well with: Half Court Soccer or 2v2v2 Keep-Away
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones
ME & THE GAME  —  Introduced: both transition moments, the instant of winning and losing
Activity 08
Flying Numbers – Transition Focus
Both Transitions · Me & the Game Introduced
Setup
Same as the opening game anchor, but now explicitly framed around transition. Outgoing players must sprint off the field. Incoming players must sprint on. No walking at any point during rotation. Call the numbers quickly so there is barely any gap between rounds.
What to Look For
The transition moment is the entire point. What happens the instant a goal is scored or the ball goes out? Do players react and sprint in, or shuffle in? Urgency in transition is a habit and habits form through repetition. This is why Flying Numbers appears in both the anchor and focus roles.
“What is the difference between a team that sprints into transition and a team that walks? How does that change what the other team can do?”
→ Pairs well with: Crisscross or Hawaii
1 ball · 2 goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 09
Crisscross
Find the Goal Instantly · Me & the Game
Setup
Square grid with a goal on the middle of each side, 4 goals total. One team scores on the two endline goals; the other scores on the two touchline goals. Play 2v2 or 3v3. Score by passing through a goal to a teammate. The cross-axis attack means defenders must constantly re-orient.
What to Look For
Trains the transition instinct directly: the instant you win the ball, find the goal immediately. Players who hesitate give defenders time to recover. Players who look up the moment they win possession and find a goal in one or two actions are showing exactly what “Me & the Game” looks like in practice.
“How quickly after winning the ball did you find your target? What would have happened if you waited two more seconds?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Hawaii
1 ball · 4 cone goals (pairs, arm’s length apart) · Pinnies
Activity 10
Hawaii
Relentless Transitions · Me & the Game
Setup
Square grid. Two goals set back about 5 yards from each end, one per team. 3v3 or 4v4. Players can score on either goal on their end line but must shoot from inside the grid. Keep extra balls next to you and feed them in immediately after each score or out-of-bounds. Continuous play, no stoppages.
What to Look For
The continuous play means players are constantly switching between attack and defense. Watch for who reacts instantly versus who celebrates and switches off. Relentless transitions make this the most game-realistic activity in this group.
“When a new ball came in while you were still celebrating a goal, how quickly did you switch back to defending?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Crisscross
Multiple balls (keep several nearby) · 4 goals in 2 pairs · Disc cones · Pinnies
INTEGRATION  —  All four focuses active simultaneously
Activity 11
Hotbox
Switch Point of Attack · All Focuses
Setup
Rectangular grid with a small goal at each end and a no-go square (the hotbox) in the center. 3v3 or 4v4. Players cannot enter the hotbox. Only balls can pass through. When attackers’ path is blocked by the hotbox and a defender on one side, the solution is to switch the ball to the other side.
What to Look For
Naturally generates all four focuses in a single activity. “Me & the Ball”: ball mastery under pressure. “Me & My Teammates”: switching the point of attack. “Me & My Team Defending”: cutting off lanes around the hotbox. “Me & the Game”: instant reaction on turnover. Best used in week 7 to observe all four without isolating any one.
“When your path was blocked, what did you do? And when the ball turned over, what was your first movement?”
→ Pairs well with: Team Handball or Five Goal Game
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones for outer grid and hotbox square · Pinnies
Activity 12
Team Handball
Assists to Score · Me & My Teammates Integration
Setup
Mark space with 4 cones, goals at each end. Two teams. Players use their hands to pass and catch. They can run with the ball but only for three steps. Score with your feet, but only after a teammate passes you the ball. No dropping to yourself. If the ball hits the ground, goes out, or is intercepted, it is a turnover.
What to Look For
Using their hands makes it easier for players to keep their heads up and see their teammates’ positions. Because scoring requires an assist, combination play is mandatory, not optional. The three-step limit creates natural transition moments. Good final-week activity before the closing game.
“Because you had to score from a pass, what did you do differently in terms of your positioning than in a normal game?”
→ Pairs well with: Hotbox or Five Goal Game
1 ball (any type) · 2 goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 13
Five Goal Game
Decision-Making · All Focuses Integration
Setup
Rectangular grid with a cone goal in each corner plus one in the center, 5 goals total. 3v3 or 4v4. Players try to pass the ball through any goal to a teammate to score in any direction. Defenders protect all 5 goals and can only gain possession by intercepting or stealing.
What to Look For
At U12 this game requires players to read the whole picture: all five goals, where teammates are, where defenders are. Watch for players who signal to a teammate where they are switching before the ball arrives. That anticipation is “Me & My Teammates” at its most sophisticated. Also watch for immediate transition reactions on every turnover.
“When you changed which goal you were going for, did your teammate know before you passed, or after?”
→ Pairs well with: Hotbox or Team Handball
1 ball · 5 goal pairs (4 corners + 1 center) · Pinnies
Closing Play
7v7 or 9v9 – Full Game
The biggest game of the session. 25 minutes. Proper goals if possible. This is where everything comes together.

The Setup

7v7 or 9v9 depending on numbers. Include a goalkeeper and rotate it so every player experiences the position across the season. No offside until players are genuinely ready for it. Let the game run without stopping.

What to Notice

Watch for the focus you introduced that week showing up without being asked for. A compact defensive shape. A player who sprints to press the instant possession is lost. On Practice B days, notice whether anything from the first session carried over.
Closing Question “Was there a moment today where your whole team did something together, attacking or defending, that one player couldn’t have done alone?”
Goalkeeper:  US Soccer recommends every player experience multiple positions across the season, and that includes goalkeeper. Rotate the position so everyone gets time in it. If a player shows genuine enthusiasm for the role, encourage that. Positional passion at this age is worth developing.
Season Plan
Sample Rotation  ·  Two Practices Per Week

Weeks 1–2 establish “Me & the Ball” and “Me & My Teammates” in the bigger 9v9 environment. Weeks 3–4 introduce team defending. Weeks 5–6 introduce transition moments. Week 7 integrates all four. Week 8 belongs to you.

Coaching Cues
What to Watch For and How to Respond
Team defends compactly

“That shape right there, everyone close enough to support each other. That is exactly what we are building toward.”

Player presses instantly after turnover

Name it in the moment: “Did everyone see that? No hesitation, immediate reaction. That is what we are building toward.”

Player switches point of attack

“That pass across the field: what did it do to the defenders? It made them run. That is the whole idea.”

Players coaching each other

Step further back. Let it run. Come back and ask: “What did you tell your teammate there?”

Concept from Practice A shows up in Practice B

Name it explicitly: “You did that in the first session this week. Your body remembered. That is exactly what two practices is for.”

Players too self-critical

“The best players in the world make mistakes in every game. The difference is what they do next.”

Player Development
Where Your Players Will Be

These are the skills we hope to see developing by the end of the season. All players develop at different rates and not every player will show all of these. That is completely normal.

Finishing moments will appear naturally in closing games as the season progresses. Let them happen and ask about them, but hold the full finishing focus for U14 where the bigger field creates richer situations.

Moving on to U14

Keeps the ball under pressure with confidence

Defends as part of a team, not just individually
