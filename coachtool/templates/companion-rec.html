U10 Coach Companion Me & the Ball  ·  Me & My Teammates  ·  Me & My Team Defending

Foundation Phase II  ·  7v7 Format  ·  2 Practices Per Week  ·  Recreational
Session Length:70–75 minutes total  ·  Arrive 15 minutes early

The game gets bigger this year. The field is wider, there are more players, and the problems are more complex. The good news is your players are ready for it. They are starting to discover what every great player knows: figuring it out with a teammate is more satisfying than figuring it out alone.

US Soccer Alignment
Foundation Phase II  ·  7v7 Format

Every activity in this guide reflects what US Soccer recommends for this age group. The 7v7 format gives players enough space and enough teammates to start experiencing those moments in a real game.

Who Is In Front of You
Understanding Your U10 Players

How They Have Grown

They have more sustained attention and can begin to understand that their actions affect teammates. They are ready to solve problems together, not just individually.

What Motivates Them

Scoring, competing, and belonging. The social side of the game intensifies at this age. They care about their teammates. Celebrate assists as loudly as goals.

The Big Shift

Passing is no longer just an option. It is becoming a tool. Players are beginning to understand that combining with a teammate can beat a defender. That insight is what this year is built around.

What They Are Ready For

2v1 situations, moving after passing, body shape to receive, and basic combination play. Set plays and formations come later. Right now, the game itself is the best teacher.

What a Great U10 Session Looks Like
Players move after passing

The give-and-go instinct is forming. Any session where players pass and then stand still is a session where that habit is not developing.

Body shape opens to the field

Players receiving with their hips at 45–90 degrees toward goal. Name it when you see it. This is a skill that takes years to become automatic.

2v1 situations recognized

Players starting to sense when they have a teammate free. They don’t always use it yet. The recognition is the first step.

Players talking to each other

Even one player calling for the ball is a win. Communication is a skill and it starts with the simplest signal.

High energy across both practices

Two practices a week means twice the opportunity to build habits. Keep both sessions varied and active. The second practice builds on the first.

Coach with intention

Pick one thing to look for before the session starts. Watch for it. When it happens, ask one good question. That is more effective than a dozen instructions from the sideline.

Before You Arrive
What Prepared Looks Like
The best sessions start before the first player arrives. Mark the fields, set up the goals, and know your two activities before anyone shows up. Practice A introduces the concept for the week. Practice B deepens it. Know which one you are running before you get there.
The Parents on the Sideline

Before the session starts, take 30 seconds to walk over and say something like:

“Today we are working on passing and moving: making a pass and then finding space to get it back. The most helpful thing you can do is cheer when they pass to a teammate, especially if they then keep running.”

Most parents want to know what is happening and how to help. Give them a role and they will take it.

Opening Play
4v4 or 5v5 — Free Small-Sided Game
Same setup every session, all season. Players should know this game before they arrive.

The Setup

Run one or two small fields simultaneously: 3v3 or 4v4. Small fields mean more touches and nowhere to hide. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeeper, no offside. Ball out: kick-in or dribble-in from the sideline. Quick restarts after every goal. Same format every session so players arrive already knowing the game.

Your Job

Come in with your Activity A already chosen based on what you want to work on this week. The opening game confirms your read and shows you how ready the group is. On Practice B days, watch specifically for whether anything from Practice A earlier in the week shows up without being asked for.
One Question — Ask it after the opening game “When you received the ball, where was your first touch? Did it help you go forward or did it take you sideways?”

Practice
Activity Toolkit — Pick Two Per Session

Run Activity A for 15 minutes, then give players a brief water break or transition before Activity B. That reset gives them a chance to catch their breath and gives you a moment to set up the next game. Activity A is the anchor: familiar, physical, high-repetition. Activity B is where the concept for the week lives.

The Pairing Rule Try to avoid pairing two new activities in the same session. Changing grids gives players a mental break, so mixing up the physical setup between Activity A and B helps too. The best pairs: one that gets hearts pumping, one that requires them to think.

Season Plan
Sample Rotation  ·  Two Practices Per Week

Each week has two sessions. Activity A anchors the session with something physical and largely familiar. Activity B introduces or deepens the concept for that week. The first two weeks focus on “Me & the Ball” under pressure in the new 7v7 environment. Weeks three through five shift the focus to “Me & My Teammates.” Weeks six and seven add “Me & My Team Defending.” Week eight is yours.

Closing Play
5v5 or 7v7 — Back to the Game
Bigger than the opening game. Game-realistic. 22 minutes. No stoppages.

The Setup

5v5 or full 7v7 if numbers allow. Proper goals or large cone goals. No offside to start. As players grow through the season and games start to look more organized, you can introduce offside in simple terms. For now, let them play freely and focus on putting their new skills to work. Quick restarts after every goal.

What to Notice

Did anything from Activity A or B show up? A player who opened their body to receive. A pass-and-move that created a goal. A 2v1 recognized and used. You do not need to name it. Noticing it is evidence the session worked. On Practice B days, check whether anything carried over from Practice A earlier in the week?
Closing Question “What is one moment today where a pass to a teammate made something happen that you couldn’t have done alone?”
Coaching Cues
What to Watch For and How to Respond
Player moves after passing

“Yes, that is exactly it. Now your teammate has an option they didn’t have before you moved.”

Player opens hips when receiving

“Did you see that? They already knew where they were going before the ball arrived. That is body shape.”

Player finds teammate in 2v1

Celebrate it clearly: “That is combination play. Two players just solved a problem that one couldn’t.”

Players bunching around the ball

Ask: “Where is the space on this field right now? Who could go find it?”

Activity A finishes flat

That is the cue to shorten Activity B or swap it for something more physical. Two activities only works if the first one actually runs well.

Concept from Practice A shows up in Practice B

Name it explicitly: “You did that earlier this week. Your body remembered.” That connection is exactly what two practices per week is for.
