Methodology

The B1–B5 Framework

Five blocks. Each one addresses a specific game problem. Together they cover everything a U11-U12 player needs to develop: individual technique, combination play, collective defending, transition speed, and finishing under pressure.

The blocks are not a checklist. They are a system. Each block builds on the ones before it, and each one is revisited across the season at a deeper level as players grow. A player working on Me and the Game (B4) is drawing on everything they developed in B1, B2, and B3. When something breaks down late in the season, the answer is usually in an earlier block.

The framework is built to US Soccer Foundation Phase III standards for U11-U12 players and is designed to be delivered by any coach willing to prepare, observe, and adjust.

How the Blocks Connect

B1 is the foundation

Everything else depends on a player’s ability to scan, receive cleanly, and play forward. If any block is breaking down, look here first.

B2 builds on B1

Combination play only works if individual technique is in place. Players who cannot control the ball under light pressure cannot execute wall passes at game speed.

B3 connects to B2

Collective defending begins the moment possession is lost. The counter-press is a direct extension of the combination work in B2, just in the opposite direction.

B4 links everything

Transition speed and game intelligence require B1, B2, and B3 to be functional. Players who react fast in transitions are drawing on all three previous blocks at once.

B5 is the output

Finishing moments are created by the work in B1 through B4. When players miss chances, the answer is rarely more shooting practice. Check the preparation first.

The blocks are sequenced across the season as a spiral: introduced, revisited, deepened. Introduction becomes Development. Development becomes Application. The work compounds.
