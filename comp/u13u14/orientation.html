How to Use This Site

This site is organized around a simple idea: players develop best when coaches understand where they are, plan where they are going, and sequence the work deliberately to get them there. Everything here is built to support that process.

If you are new to this framework, start with the Coach Education pages. They will give you the context you need before the methodology makes full sense. If you are already familiar with how we work, jump straight to the block or season plan you need.

Introduction, Development, Application
Level 1

Introduction

Players are becoming aware of a concept and trying it. Decisions are slow and execution is inconsistent. Every player at every position works through this level the same way, it is universal.

Level 2

Development

Players are making repeatable, intentional choices. They understand the concept and are beginning to apply it under pressure, but it is not yet automatic. Still universal, no position differentiation yet.

Level 3

Application

Decisions are fast and reliable under real game pressure. This is where position-specific depth begins to show up, grouped by who actually executes the action together, not by fixed labels.

Your goal is not to push every player to Application by the end of the season. Your goal is to recognize where each player is and create the right conditions for them to move forward from there. Some players will arrive at Introduction and leave at Development. That is progress. Some will arrive at Development and reach Application under pressure by the final weeks. That is a great season.

At U13, there is one more layer worth understanding. Introduction and Development stay universal in every block, every player needs the same foundational habits regardless of position. Application is where specialization begins, grouped by function rather than assigned upfront. A player's technique and decision-making at Application are still revealing what kind of player he is becoming. Locking a player into a position too early risks missing who he actually turns out to be.

The Blocks

The five blocks each address a specific game problem. They are not drills or exercises. They are the environments and challenges players need to develop the skills that show up in matches.

Me and the Ball (B1) is the foundation: scanning, first touch, body shape, playing forward under pressure. Everything else builds from it, and it stays position-blind at every level. If a player is struggling in Me and My Team Defending (B3) or Me and the Game (B4), look back at Me and the Ball first.

The blocks are sequenced across the season as a spiral, revisited at deeper levels as players grow. The season plan shows you exactly how that sequencing works week by week, along with a weekly theme you can build session content and Soccer IQ study around.

B1
Me and the Ball
Perception, first touch, protecting, driving, dribbling, passing, decision-making under U13 game pressure
› B2
Me and My Teammates
Combination play, supporting angles, overloads, CB penetration, midfield interchange
› B3
Me and My Team Defending
Pressure-cover-balance, compact high line, coordinated pressing, defending is not just for defenders
› B4
Me and the Game
Transition speed, recognizing the moment, press vs. recover, play forward vs. secure
› B5
Me and the Moment
Finishing, final actions, composure and game management built through repetition
›
The Season Plan

The season plan lays out all 18 weeks across three phases, Preseason, In-Season, and Peak Performance, showing which blocks and levels run each week and why. Each week also carries a theme, a mental-and-technical thread you can build a session or a Soccer IQ discussion around, separate from the underlying block sequencing.

A Note on This Framework

This methodology is built to US Soccer Progression Phase I standards for U13-U14 players.
