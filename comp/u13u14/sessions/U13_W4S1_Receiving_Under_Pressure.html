Receiving Under Pressure
Week 4 · Session 1 · Monday
B1 — Me & the BallDevelopmentCombined teamsReceiving
Philosophy Connection

The field teaches the half turn before we name it. Corner goals with open scoring remove every safe retreat, so a player who receives square or flat-footed has nowhere to go. Players feel the problem first, then we give it a name. The technical block then makes the scan and the directional first touch a repeatable habit before it comes back under pressure in the games.

Session Objectives
  • Scan before receiving
  • Use a directional first touch to exit pressure
  • Receive on the half turn, not square
  • Choose the open exit when one is cut off

ObjectiveEvery reception requires a scan and a directional first touch to exit pressure. The field teaches the half turn before we name it.
Coach Note

Corner goals and open scoring create omnidirectional pressure. There is no safe end to retreat to and no predictable direction pressure comes from. Players who receive square or flat-footed have nowhere to go. Do not name the half turn here. Let the problem show up first.

Coaching Points
  • Scan before the ball arrives. Where is pressure coming from?
  • Receive on the half turn, not square
  • First touch takes you out of pressure

Vocabulary
Half Turn
Receiving with the body open to the field, not square to the passer, so the first touch can go forward or away from pressure.
When: introduce it the first time it appears in the SSG or the square of four

Question 1
