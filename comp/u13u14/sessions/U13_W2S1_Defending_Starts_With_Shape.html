Defending Starts With Shape
Week 2 · Session 1 · Monday
120 minB3 — Me & My Team DefendingIntroductionCombined teamsDefend  ·  Defending Third
Philosophy Connection

Effort without shape is just chasing. A player who sprints to close the ball but leaves their cover position open has worked hard and made things worse. Today we install the idea that defending is a collective action: one player pressures, a teammate covers, the shape stays connected. We start with the individual decision (press or delay) and build outward to the first collective concept (pressure and cover). The vocabulary introduced today stays in use for the rest of the season.

Session Objectives
  • Read the receiving touch and make a press or delay decision based on what you see
  • Stay touch tight when the moment calls for it
  • Push the attacker outside and protect the central space
  • Understand the difference between the pressure and cover roles
  • Communicate cover position so the pressing defender can commit fully
  • Connect individual defending habits into a two and three-player unit

Coach Note

Introduce Touch Tight, Press, and Delay when the action appears, not before the activity starts. The scoring tells players what they are protecting without you explaining it.

Coaching Points
  • Watch the touch first. Heavy touch, press. Clean touch, delay.
  • Touch tight: close enough that a bad touch loses the ball. Not diving in, just close.
  • Push outside. The central lane is the most dangerous space.
  • Defender wins the ball: play through the gate. Recognize the transition, go.

ObjectiveAdd one defender, add one concept. Pressure applies everything from Activity 3. Cover protects the inside lane, does not follow the attacker outside, and calls their position so pressure can commit.
Coach Note

Communication is the point of this activity. The pressure defender cannot fully commit until the cover defender has announced they are there. Introduce "Pressure" and "Cover" the moment the two-defender shape appears. Ask the cover defender: where are you, and did your partner hear you?

Coaching Points
  • No call, no commitment.
  • Cover protects the inside lane. They do not follow the attacker outside.
  • Pressure uses the same read as Activity 3: touch first, then decision.
  • Think of it as the 4 and 2, or the 5 and 3. One steps, one covers behind.

U10 goal

ObjectiveThree defenders function as a connected unit against two attackers. Without support, pressure and cover can still be beaten by a simple combination.
Coach Note

Rotate groups so everyone gets reps at all three roles. At Introduction level the goal is understanding that the third defender has a specific job. If the shape connects and players are communicating the three words, that is success today.

The Three Roles
PressureOn the ball. Read the touch, stay touch tight, push outside, deny the forward pass.
CoverBehind the pressure defender at the correct angle. Protect the inside lane. Call your position.
SupportStay connected to cover. Protect the space the second attacker could receive in. Do not ball-watch.
Coaching Points
  • Three defenders, three jobs. Everyone has a role before the ball is played in.
  • Support: your job is the second attacker, not the ball.
  • All three words should be audible: pressure, cover, support. If it is silent it is broken.
  • Shape stays connected. If one defender chases, the other two adjust.
  • When the ball is won: recognize the moment, play out to the end line, go.

ObjectiveNo conditions, no roles assigned. The test is whether pressure, cover, and touch tight show up without being forced. Watch for communication, the press or delay decision, and cover position appearing when a teammate steps. This is data for next session.
Coaching Points
  • Is anyone calling "pressure" or "cover" without being prompted?
  • Is the press or delay decision being made, or are defenders just reacting?
  • Name it out loud when the shape holds: "that is pressure and cover, did you feel that?"
Vocabulary
Touch Tight
The defender is close enough that a heavy first touch loses the ball. Not diving in, just close enough that a bad touch ends the attack.
When: defender is within a step of the ball carrier
Press
The touch was heavy or the attacker is off balance. Win the ball now.
When: visual trigger fires, heavy touch or closed body shape
Delay
The touch was clean. Stay goal-side, wait for the trigger.
When: attacker has control, no clear press trigger yet
Pressure / Cover
First collective defending concept. One pressures the ball, one covers the dangerous space behind. Cover calls their position so pressure can commit.
When: any 1v2 or larger defending situation

What tells you whether to press or delay?
