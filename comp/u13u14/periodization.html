Why We Periodize

Periodization ties every week of training to a developmental standard, what U13 players actually need to be building at this point in their growth, sequenced in the order that lets those habits compound. It’s not a guess about what to work on. It’s a plan built against a framework, and it holds regardless of how last Saturday went.

Compare that to the alternative: after a tough loss, it’s tempting to chase the scoreline. Conceded on transitions, so Tuesday becomes a transition drill. Missed a few chances, so Wednesday is finishing. It feels responsive, but it treats symptoms instead of building players, and nothing compounds.

A periodized plan stays with the standard instead. Trust the sequence, and problems like transitions and finishing tend to solve themselves, because the foundation underneath them is solid.

How It Works

The blocks do not run once and finish. The season is designed as a spiral: a concept is introduced, the team moves to other work, then returns to that concept at a deeper level, with real spacing between reps so each return visit is a genuine retention check, not just a continuation. Each time a block reappears, players bring more experience to it. Introduction becomes Development. Development becomes Application. At U13, Application is also where specialization begins, grouped by who executes the action together rather than assigned upfront. The work compounds.

The three phases describe the overall arc of that spiral across the fall season.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1-6

Preseason

No Application-level work yet, there is no real pressure to apply anything against until games start. Players install and repeat the universal habits and technique that every later block depends on: individual technique, combination play, and collective defending shape.

Phase 2 · Weeks 7-12

In-Season

Games provide the pressure that Application requires. Transitions and finishing come online, while individual technique, combination play, and defending begin their Application reps against real defenders instead of training constraints.

Phase 3 · Weeks 13-18

Peak Performance

Not mastery, the point in the season where players are the fittest and best trained they will be before the break. Every block cycles at Application through repeated performance, not new content.

Weekly Themes

Alongside the block sequencing, every week in the season plan carries a weekly theme, a mental-and-technical thread separate from the underlying block logic. Where the block sequence tells you which skill area is being installed or reinforced, the theme gives you something a player can actually hold onto: a focus word or idea you can build a session around, discuss before or after training, or use to anchor a Soccer IQ chapter or Strikers Study topic.

The themes aren't a second curriculum. They're a lens on the same week's work, connecting the technical content to something bigger, tempo, composure, decision-making, ownership, so players experience the block work as part of a larger idea, not just a drill.

Why This Protects Your Players

A coach who reacts to every result is unintentionally sending a message: what we are working on depends on whether we won last week. Players feel that instability. They start playing not to make mistakes rather than playing to get better.

A periodized plan sends a different message. We know where we are going. We know what we are building. This week’s session connects to last week’s and next week’s. Players develop confidence not just in their skills but in the process itself.

What It Asks of You

It asks you to hold the line when the instinct to fix things is strong. You will have sessions after difficult games where you want to throw the plan out and run transition drills for 90 minutes. Sometimes that is the right call. But the default should always be the plan, because the plan was built when you were thinking clearly, not in the 24 hours after a difficult loss.

Every week in the season plan has a purpose. Every block connects to the next. The work you do in the early weeks shows up in the final ones.

“The deeper players love this game, the harder they work. The habits built in the first weeks of a season show up in the last ones.”
