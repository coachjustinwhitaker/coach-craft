U13–U14 Player Development

Coach Craft

Coach Education

Who Is in Front of Us

Before we talk about what to teach, we need to talk about who we are teaching. U13-U14 is an inflection point, socially, emotionally, cognitively, and physically. Understanding what is happening to your players off the pitch is just as important as understanding what you want them to do on it, and it is the foundation for why this season's methodology looks different from last year's.

Social

These players are transitioning into an entirely new social environment, the kind of shift that comes with moving toward high school. They are actively asserting their individuality and forming their own identity, which can create real friction with authority, including you. They are heavily dependent on a peer group or a best friend, and they feel the pull between standing out as an individual and fitting in with the group. Pop culture, sports culture, and social media now shape them as much as their parents do.

What this means for coaching

This is the age where players start pushing back, not because they have stopped caring, but because pushing back is part of how they are building an identity. A coach who treats this as defiance will lose them. A coach who treats it as a normal step in growing up, and gives players some ownership of decisions, will keep them engaged through the friction.

Emotional

Players are newly sensitive about their appearance and physical changes, often comparing themselves to teammates who are maturing faster or slower. Mood swings are sharper than at U12, impulse control and emotional maturity are still catching up to the intensity of what they feel. They are still impulsive, often reacting before they have thought through consequences, and they can feel embarrassed if parents are too visible in the social setting. Anxiety about uncertain outcomes is more present at this age than before.

What this means for coaching

The blowup after a bad touch or a tough loss is not an attitude problem. It is a player who has not yet built the regulation skills to match the intensity of the emotion. More is now genuinely at stake for them socially than it was at U12, which means losses and mistakes carry more weight than they used to.

Cognitive

This is the inflection point. These players can now form abstractions and generalizations that apply across different situations, things like justice, fairness, and team identity. They can use their own reasoning to move beyond what they are told and form their own conclusions. They are beginning to take responsibility for their own commitment level and can plan ahead with little to no adult guidance. Most importantly for this team: they can start pursuing expert knowledge in a specific domain and develop an identity around it. Not just "a soccer player" but "the center back" or "the wing back."

What this means for coaching

As cognitive ability deepens, so does a player's capacity to handle complexity and own responsibility. This is what makes deeper positional work possible at U13. A player isn't just being told where to stand anymore. They can understand why that responsibility exists, what it demands of them, and start to own it for themselves. As we build more positional depth into training, we're matching it to a level of thinking these players are only now capable of.

Physical

Rapid growth is now visible, increases in height, weight, and muscle mass that show up differently across the roster. Players will have different rates of neuromuscular strength, height, and weight changes depending on where they are in puberty. This can create real physical awkwardness as bodies change quickly, and injury rates tend to rise during this stage, ankle sprains, tendonitis, patellofemoral pain, and growth-related issues like Osgood-Schlatters and Sever's Disease.

What this means for coaching

The gap between your earliest and latest maturing players is wider this year than it was last year, and it will keep widening through U14. A player who looks physically dominant right now may not be the most technically or tactically advanced player, and a smaller player who looks behind physically may be ahead of the group cognitively and technically. Don't let physical maturity stand in for development.

That variability also means physical effort expectations can't be universal. A running standard, a recovery expectation, a fitness benchmark, all of it needs to flex to where each individual player actually is in their physical development. And build in attention to load management, this is the age where overuse injuries start to show up if training volume isn't watched.

What US Soccer Asks of This Age Group

Progression Phase I — U13-U14 Development Goals

The US Soccer Player Development Framework moves this age group into what it calls Progression Phase I, the first stage where players are expected to play within a formation with defined roles and responsibilities. New objectives appear here that didn't exist at U12: midfield interchange, center back penetrations into midfield to create overloads, and quick combination play under high pressure in tight spaces, a deliberate step up from the pressure level expected at U12.

US Soccer's coaching guidance for this phase asks coaches to help players take ownership of their development, encourage player-led warmups and player-to-player coaching, and push players to solve game situations systematically rather than relying on adult instruction in the moment.

Source: US Soccer Player Development Framework, Progression Phase I, U13-U14 Learning Plan (2023)
