Progression Phase I  ·  11v11  ·  2 Practices Per Week  ·  Recreational
Session Length:Up to 90 minutes total  ·  Arrive 15 minutes early

The field got big. Eleven against eleven means shape matters as much as skill. The players in front of you can reason now, they care what their teammates think, and they want a say. Your job this season is to give them real problems to solve in games they enjoy, then step back when they solve them. Enjoy watching them take ownership.

What Each Focus Means at U14
Me & the Ball
Growth Phase
Scanning, first touch, body shape and playing forward. Players know these habits and now use them at full-game speed with a defender closing fast. Every player does this, in every position.
Me & My Teammates
Growth Phase
Support angles, wall passes and moving after the pass. New this season is timing: a run that arrives when the ball does, not early and not late.
Me & My Team Defending
Reinforced
Pressure, cover and balance with eleven. The nearest player pressures, a teammate covers behind, and the team stays close enough to stay connected. Forwards defend too.
Me & the Game
Reinforced
The first second after the ball changes hands. Press or recover when you lose it. Play forward or keep it when you win it.
Me & the Moment
Introduced
Finishing: separating to receive, a final pass, and a composed shot with few touches. New at U14 and the focus of weeks 4 to 6.
US Soccer Alignment
Progression Phase I  ·  11v11 Format

At U14, US Soccer’s Progression Phase I expects players to play within a formation with defined roles, and adds new objectives like midfield interchange and quick combination play in tight spaces under high pressure. For a recreational team, take the spirit and keep it light. Teach the shape of the team and the habits that go with it, and let players try different spots across the season. Finishing joins the first four focuses as a fifth. All five run at the Introduction level.

What This Season Keeps Simple

Introduction level in all five focuses. The habits every player needs in every position. No fixed roles. Players rotate through lines and positions, because identity at this age is still forming.

What a Competitive Program Adds

Development and Application levels, where the same ideas run at full game speed and then by function: who combines together, who presses together, who finishes. Press triggers, center backs stepping into midfield and role-specific work all live there.
Who Is In Front of You
Understanding Your U14 Players

Growth Is Uneven

In any U14 group you will see players who have already grown a lot and players who have not started. Do not read size as skill. Praise decisions and effort, and watch that your biggest players are not only winning duels with size. Sore knees or heels are worth a word with the parent. Rest beats extra reps.

The Field Got Big

The jump from 9v9 to 11v11 is a jump in distance, not just numbers. Players tire faster and shape breaks down. Short rests and water breaks are coaching tools, not weaknesses.

Peers Matter More Than You

At this age what teammates think carries a lot of weight. Individual feedback works best quietly, one on one. Public praise for team moments and brave decisions builds the culture you want. Pushing back is part of building an identity, not disrespect. Give them a say: let players pick a warm-up game or lead one.

They Can Think It Through

U14 players can reason about why, not just what. Ask “what was open?” or “why did we lose our shape there?” and let them answer before you do. A solution they work out themselves sticks.

What a Great U14 Session Looks Like
Players take the shot

The ball is in the final third and someone shoots, early, with composure, without a second dribble. Missing is fine. Not shooting is the problem.

Compact, connected shape

Eleven players on a big field can look scattered. Look for lines that stay close enough to support each other: defenders, midfielders and forwards moving as one unit.

Instant reaction on turnovers

When the ball changes hands, do players react in the first second or watch? That reaction is the whole game-moments block.

Players talking to each other

Voices on the field: “Man on,” “Turn,” “Switch.” Player-to-player coaching is a development goal. Step back when it happens.

Practice A concept in Practice B

If something from the first session of the week shows up in the second without you asking, the concept is taking hold.

Everyone gets a turn in goal

Rotate the goalkeeper. Finishing practice is also goalkeeper practice, so nobody should miss out on a turn.

Teaching Points for This Age Group
What You Are Developing
ControllingProtectingDriving DribblingPassingShooting FinishingScanningSupporting Body ShapeCoveringMarking Timing Runs

Finishing and Timing Runs are the new pieces this season. Finishing is shooting with composure when it matters: choosing the corner, striking cleanly, and following up. Timing a run means arriving in the right place at the right moment, not early and not late. Both are what the finishing block looks like in action.

Before You Arrive
What Prepared Looks Like
Preparation at U14 is simpler than at U12, because the group already knows the language. Pick the week’s focus before you arrive and choose Activity A as something familiar. Activity B is where the new idea lives. If the group needs more time on a concept, run the revisit instead of moving on. With two practices a week, Practice A introduces and Practice B deepens. Both are correct. Try to avoid pairing two new concepts in the same session.
The Parents on the Sideline

Before the session starts, take 30 seconds to walk over. Something like:

“This season we are working on finishing and on the moment the ball changes hands. The most helpful thing you can do is cheer the brave decisions: the player who shoots, the player who sprints back, the player who tries the pass that might not work.”

At U14 players hear everything. Positive sideline energy is genuinely useful. Criticism of a missed shot makes the next shot less likely.

Opening Play
6v6 or 7v7 – Free Small-Sided Game
Same setup every session. Smaller than the closing game. More touches, more shots and cleaner decisions.

The Setup

Same setup every session. Smaller than the closing game, so more touches, more shots and cleaner decisions. Half a field, with a full-size or large goal at each end. Teams of 6 or 7. Add a goalkeeper if you have one willing. Quick restarts. Let them play the first 8 minutes without saying anything. On Practice B days, watch for whether the concept from Practice A earlier in the week shows up.

Your Job

Watch the moments in front of goal. Do players shoot? Do they pick their moment or force it? Watch the transitions too. Come in with Activity A already chosen and let the opening play confirm your read for Activity B.
One Question — Ask it after the opening game “When you had a chance to shoot today, what made you take it or pass it up?”
1 ball per team · Full-size or large goals · Disc cones · Pinnies in 2 colors
Practice
Activity Toolkit – Pick Two Per Session

Run Activity A for 15 minutes, then Activity B for 15 minutes. A brief transition or water break (“good work, new game”) resets attention and energy. Follow the season plan for which focus to prioritize each week. The five opening games at the top of the toolkit replace the free small-sided game when a week calls for a challenge built into the play.

The Pairing Rule Try to avoid pairing two new concepts in the same session. Activity A works best as something players already know well enough to run without much explanation. Activity B is where the new concept lives. If the group is flat after Activity A, shorten Activity B or swap it for something more physical.
OPENING PLAY  —  One added rule sets the challenge before the practice starts
Activity 01
3v3 Everybody Plays
Passing · Supporting · Combination Play · Me & My Teammates
Setup
Keep it small. Mark a field about 35 by 30 yards. Go smaller if it plays loose. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeepers. 3v3. Extra players wait on the sideline and rotate in after each goal. Have each team count its touches out loud, one, two, three, so everyone knows who has played.
What to Look For
Show for the ball. Be somewhere your teammate can see you. After you pass, move to a new spot. Find the teammate who has not touched it yet.
“How did you let your teammate know you were free?”
Balls · Cones · Mini goals or cone goals · Pinnies in 2 colors
Activity 02
4v4 Two in the Final Third
Compact Shape · Pressing · Numerical Advantage · Me & My Team Defending
Setup
Keep it small. Mark a field about 35 by 30 yards. Go smaller if it plays loose. Use cones to mark the final third at each end, about a third of the field. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeepers. 4v4. Extra players wait on the sideline and rotate in after each goal. Walk the final-third lines once so players see where the limit is.
What to Look For
Defenders: stay close together. Shrink the space. Attackers: nothing there? Pass it back and reset. Win it, then go. Attack the other way right away.
“How close were you to your teammates when you won it?”
Balls · Cones · Mini goals or cone goals · Pinnies in 2 colors
Activity 03
4v4 Win It and Go
Decision-Making · Pressing · Combination Play · Me & the Game
Setup
Keep it small. Mark a field about 35 by 30 yards. Go smaller if it plays loose. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeepers. 4v4. Extra players wait on the sideline and rotate in after each goal. Tell players to start the count the moment they win the ball.
What to Look For
Win it, look up and go. Pass forward early, to the open player. Lost it? Get back and win it again.
“What did you see the moment you won it?”
Balls · Cones · Mini goals or cone goals · Pinnies in 2 colors
Activity 04
5v5 First-Time Finish
Shooting · Decision-Making · Combination Play · Me & the Moment
Setup
Keep it small. Mark a field about 40 by 30 yards. Go smaller if it plays loose. A large goal or cone goal at each end. Add a goalkeeper if you have one willing. 5v5. Extra players wait on the sideline and rotate in after each goal. Make the goals as big as you can, so players take shots.
What to Look For
Get your body ready before the ball arrives. Move into the space where the cutback or the pass will arrive. Missed? Reset right away.
“What did you do before the pass reached you?”
Balls · Cones · Mini goals or cone goals · Pinnies in 2 colors
Activity 05
5v4 With a Floater
Numerical Advantage · Passing · Possession · Me & My Teammates
Setup
Keep it small. Mark a field about 35 by 30 yards. Go smaller if it plays loose. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeepers. Two teams of four, plus one floater who plays for whichever team has the ball. Extra players wait on the sideline and rotate in after each goal. Give the floater a different color pinnie from both teams.
What to Look For
Use the floater when you are under pressure. Move into a spot where the defense cannot see you and the ball. Look for the free player before you pass.
“When was the floater free, and did you find them?”
Balls · Cones · Mini goals or cone goals · Pinnies in 2 colors
ME & THE BALL  —  Ball skills at game speed
Activity 06
Flying Numbers
Decision-Making · Me & the Ball
Setup
Mark a space with cones, goals on both sides. Two teams along the touchline either side of you. Call a number combination and each side sends in that many players to scrimmage. New players rotate in after every goal or ball out. Outgoing players sprint off, incoming players sprint on. No walking at any point.
What to Look For
Your anchor activity every week. Players know it from U12, so it needs almost no explanation and it settles the group fast. At U14, call uneven numbers (3v2, 4v3) and watch what the extra player does. If one player is dominating, play them 1v2.
“When your team had the extra player, what did you do with that advantage?”
1 ball · 2 goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
ME & MY TEAMMATES  —  Combination play, switching, patience
Activity 07
Barcelona
Possession · Passing · Combination Play · Me & My Teammates
Setup
Rectangular grid with goals at each end. 4v4, no goalkeeper. Score points equal to the number of passes completed before scoring: 8 passes before a goal is 8 points. A missed shot scores nothing. Play short rounds of 3 to 4 minutes.
What to Look For
Watch for players who switch the ball to the far side before going to goal, drawing defenders across before finding the opening. Name it when it happens. Best as a focus activity, not an anchor.
“Before you scored, was there a moment you switched the ball to the other side? What did that open up?”
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 08
Hotbox
Combination Play · Decision-Making · Me & My Teammates
Setup
Rectangular grid with a small goal at each end and a no-go square (the hotbox) in the center. 4v4 or 5v5. Players cannot enter the hotbox. Only the ball can pass through it. When the attackers’ path is blocked by the hotbox and a defender, the solution is to switch the ball to the other side.
What to Look For
Generates all five blocks in a single game: ball skills under pressure, switching with teammates, cutting off lanes around the box, instant reaction on turnovers, and a decisive finish when the opening appears. Best used in week 7 to observe without isolating any one block. Do not over-coach. Watch.
“When your path was blocked, what did you do? And when the ball turned over, what was your first movement?”
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones for outer grid and hotbox square · Pinnies
ME & MY TEAM DEFENDING  —  Compact shape, first and second defender
Activity 09
Shrinking Field: Defending Focus
Compact Shape · Pressing · Me & My Team Defending
Setup
Rectangular grid with small goals at each end. 4v4 or 5v5. Every minute, move each touchline in by 3 steps. Tell them before you start: “today we are watching the defending team.” Play 2 to 3 rounds.
What to Look For
As the field shrinks, compact shape matters more and more. Watch for the defending team getting tighter: closer together, higher up, forcing play to one side. Are they reacting to the ball or anticipating where it is going? Anticipation is worth naming.
“As the field got smaller, did defending get easier or harder? What does that tell you about defending in tight spaces?”
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones
ME & THE GAME  —  Both transitions, relentless reaction
Activity 10
Crisscross
Decision-Making · Scanning · Me & the Game
Setup
Square grid with a goal in the middle of each side, 4 goals total. One team scores on the two endline goals, the other on the two touchline goals. Play 3v3 or 4v4. Score by passing through a goal to a teammate. The cross-axis attack forces defenders to re-orient constantly.
What to Look For
The instant a team wins the ball, do they look up and find a target within one or two actions? Players who hesitate give defenders time to recover. That first-second decision is the whole transition idea.
“How quickly after winning the ball did you find your target? What would have happened if you waited two more seconds?”
1 ball · 4 cone goals (pairs, arm’s length apart) · Pinnies
ME & THE MOMENT  —  Finishing, final pass, timing the run
Activity 11
Duels to Goal
Shooting · Decision-Making · Me & the Moment
Setup
Two lines of players about 25 yards from a full-size goal with a goalkeeper (rotate keepers often). The coach plays a ball in. The first attacker and first defender race to it and play 1v1 to goal. Shot, save, miss or ball out ends the duel. Next pair goes. Keep the pace quick: three or four duels per player, then swap roles.
What to Look For
The moment of decision: does the attacker commit, or hesitate and give the defender time? Look for the attacker who takes one decisive touch toward goal, and for the player who shoots early instead of dribbling one extra time. For defenders, look for patience: delay, do not dive in.
“When you had the ball facing the goalkeeper, what did you decide first: shoot, dribble, or pass? What told you that?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Waves
Balls in a bag · 1 full-size goal · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 12
Waves
Numerical Advantage · Passing · Shooting · Me & the Moment
Setup
Full-size goal with a goalkeeper. Three attackers start from the halfway line against two defenders and attack in waves. When the wave finishes (goal, save, or ball out), the three attackers become the next wave’s defenders, and the two defenders go to the back of the attacking line. Coach feeds a new ball in right away so play is continuous.
What to Look For
Numbers-up decisions are the heart of this one. Watch for the attacker who carries the ball until a defender commits, then passes. Watch for the third attacker arriving late into the box. Celebrate a good final pass as loudly as the goal.
“With three against two, who decided when to pass and when to shoot? How did you know?”
→ Pairs well with: Duels to Goal or Wide Service Finishing
Balls in a bag · 1 full-size goal · Disc cones · Pinnies in 2 colors
Activity 13
Wide Service Finishing
Shooting · Supporting · Me & the Moment
Setup
Full-size goal with a goalkeeper. Wide players on each flank serve balls into the box while two or three attackers arrive to finish. Start with serves along the ground or driven low, finish with feet. Rotate servers, finishers and the goalkeeper every few turns. Heading is optional: if you include it, use a lighter ball, toss to the head from short range, and keep the total number of headers very low.
What to Look For
Do the attackers time their runs so they arrive as the ball does, rather than standing in the box waiting? Do they attack the near post and far post with different players? Watch for the serve itself: low and hard, away from the goalkeeper, is hard to defend.
“When the cross came in, how did you decide where to run? What did your teammate do differently from you?”
→ Pairs well with: Waves or Flying Numbers
Balls in a bag · 1 full-size goal · Disc cones for the wide channels · Pinnies
Closing Play
11v11 – Full Game
The biggest game of the session. 25 minutes. This is where everything comes together.

The Setup

The biggest game of the session. Full field, full-size goals, goalkeepers. Play 11v11 if you have the numbers. Short of players? Play 9v9 or 10v10 on a narrower field rather than leaving gaps. Alternatively, put a full-size goal with a keeper at one end and two small goals at the halfway line for the other team to attack. Offside applies, so introduce it early in the season and be patient with it. Let the game run without stopping.

What to Notice

Watch for the focus you introduced that week showing up without being asked: a team that stays compact across three lines, a player who sprints to press the instant possession is lost, a forward who shoots when the chance comes.
Closing Question “Was there a moment today where your whole team did something together, attacking or defending, that one player could not have done alone?”
Full field · Full-size goals · Disc cones · Pinnies in 2 colors
Goalkeeper:  Every player should experience goalkeeper at some point, and at U14 finishing practice is also goalkeeper practice. Rotate the position so nobody misses a turn. If a player shows genuine enthusiasm for the role, encourage that.
Season Plan
Sample Rotation  ·  Two Practices Per Week

Weeks 1–2 bring combination play and team defending into the 11v11 environment. Week 3 revisits both transition moments. Weeks 4–6 introduce and deepen finishing, the new focus at U14. Week 7 integrates all five. Week 8 belongs to you. Each week starts with a small opening game that builds the week’s challenge into the play.

Coaching Cues
What to Watch For and How to Respond
Player shoots early and misses

“Good decision. Great shot to take. Take that one every time.” Praise the decision before you talk about the technique.

Player passes up a clear shot

Ask, do not tell: “What were you thinking when you had that space?” Then listen. Often it is confidence, not judgment.

Team defends compactly

“That shape right there, everyone close enough to support each other. That is exactly what we are building toward.”

Player presses instantly after turnover

Name it in the moment: “Did everyone see that? No hesitation. That is the whole idea.”

Players coaching each other

Step further back. Let it run. Come back and ask: “What did you tell your teammate there?”

Players too self-critical

“The best players in the world miss shots and lose the ball in every game. The difference is what they do next.”

Player Development
Where Your Players Will Be

These are the skills we hope to see developing by the end of the season. All players develop at different rates and not every player will show all of these. That is completely normal.

Finishing is the new piece this season. Let it show up in games, ask about it, and celebrate the brave shot as much as the goal.

Moving on to the Next Level

Keeps the ball under pressure on the full field
Defends as part of an eleven-player team
Reacts in the first second after the ball changes hands
Takes shots with composure and makes good final-third decisions
Times runs to arrive when the ball does
Communicates with and coaches teammates on the field
Shows up wanting to play again
