U8 Coach Companion
Me & the Ball

Foundation Phase I  ·  5v5 Format  ·  Recreational
Session Length:55–60 minutes total  ·  Arrive 15 minutes early

It's not about winning. It's not about perfect technique, or set plays, or tactical awareness. This season has one job: make sure every player leaves the pitch wanting to come back. Kids who finish Saturday morning thinking they can't wait for next week. That is the whole thing. If you find yourself enjoying it too, that happens more than you'd expect.

US Soccer Alignment
Foundation Phase I  ·  5v5 Format

Every activity in this guide reflects what US Soccer recommends for this age group: high touch counts, small-sided games, fun environments, and lots of goals. You are delivering a program that meets national standards and is designed specifically for where U8 players are in their development.

Who Is In Front of You
Understanding Your U8 Players

How They Learn

Through experience and repetition, not explanation. Show them a game and they figure it out. Keep instructions short, one idea at a time, then let them play.

What Motivates Them

Scoring goals. Being successful. Having fun with friends. Competing. Keep score — they care about it, and it creates genuine intensity without you manufacturing it.

Attention and Activity

Their attention span is short but improving. Long lines and waiting kill engagement. Every player should be active for virtually the entire session. If kids are standing still, change the game.

Positions and the Game

There are no fixed positions at U8. Every player plays everywhere over the course of the season. In the 5v5 game, rotate the goalkeeper regularly so everyone gets a turn in goal.

What a Great U8 Session Looks Like
Players have the ball constantly

We encourage players to have the ball at their feet as frequently as possible. One player, one ball is the target during practice activities. No sharing, no waiting.

Lots of goals scored

US Soccer is explicit: set up games where players can be successful and score lots of goals. Frequent scoring means high engagement and happy players.

Noise and energy

Laughing, celebrating, competing. If it is quiet, something is wrong. This age group should be loud and joyful.

Coach mostly watching

Your best coaching is a single question after something happens. Resist the urge to over-coach. The game is the teacher.

Players making decisions

Even simple ones: which goal to attack, when to dribble, when to pass. Decision-making in the game starts here.

Sessions end too soon

If players leave wanting more, you did your job. That desire to return is what builds a long-term love of the game.

Teaching Points for This Age Group
What You Are Developing
Controlling Protecting Driving Dribbling Passing Shooting Scanning Supporting Body Shape

Navy pills are technical skills — what the body does with the ball. Gold pills are awareness skills — scanning means looking up to see what is around you before the ball arrives. Every great player in the world does this, and you are starting to teach it at age seven. Pick one focus per session and let the game build the rest.

Before You Arrive
What Prepared Looks Like
The best sessions start before the first player arrives. Coaches who show up knowing their activity and their question spend the whole session watching their players and enjoying it. The setup is the same every week: mark a grid with disc cones, lay a ball out for every player, set up cone goals at each end of the closing game space. By the time players arrive, the only thing left to do is put a ball at their feet.
The Parents on the Sideline

Before the session starts, take 30 seconds to walk over and say something like:

“Today we are working on keeping the ball close and looking up while dribbling. The most helpful thing you can do is cheer everything — the attempt, the stumble, the scramble. That's the job.”

Most parents want to know what is happening and how to help. Give them a role and they will take it. If you see a parent who is genuinely enthusiastic and engaged, that conversation is worth having — the club always has room for coaches who care.

Opening Play
5v5 — Free Small-Sided Game
Same setup every week. Players should know this game before they arrive.  |  Alternative: Capture the Balls (see Activity 12)

The Setup

Mini goals or cone goals at each end. Teams of 5, including a goalkeeper. No offside. Ball out of play: restart with a kick-in or dribble-in from the sideline. Set up the space, then step back. Let them play. Restart quickly after every goal. Rotate the goalkeeper throughout the season so every player gets time in the position.

Your Job

Watch. Look for moments of good scanning, a player who tries to beat someone, a clever pass. Make a mental note of two or three things. One of them will tell you which practice activity to run today. Do not stop the game. Do not instruct. Save your voice for the question.
One Question — Ask it after the first game “Who scored a goal today? How did you do it — did you dribble past someone, or did you find a teammate?”
⚽ 1 ball per team 🔴 Mini goals or cone goals at each end 🟠 Disc cones for boundaries 🟡 Pinnies — 2 colors
Practice
Activity Toolkit — Pick One Per Session

Choose one activity based on what you saw in the opening game. Run it for 20–25 minutes. Rotate through the toolkit across the season so players build a full set of skills. Activities are grouped by skill focus.

DRIVING & BALL SKILLS  —  Moving with the ball: control, speed, and direction
Activity 01
Red Light Green Light
Driving · Agility
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards. Every player has a ball. Green light: dribble fast. Yellow light: slow down with small touches to keep the ball close. Red light: stop with one foot on the ball. Call lights at random as players move around the grid. After a minute, ask players to suggest a new color and assign it a new action — orange means swing your arms, purple means dance — and repeat each new color a few times.
What to Look For
Players keeping the ball within arm's reach at yellow. Heads up to avoid collisions. On red light, emphasize using the sole of the foot to stop rather than letting the ball roll away.
“When I called yellow, what did you have to do with your feet to keep the ball close?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for grid
Activity 02
101 Dalmatians
Dribbling · Scanning
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards with small cone triangles scattered throughout — aim for one triangle per player. Every player has a ball. Players score by dribbling through a triangle with the ball within arm's reach of their body. Players count their own score out loud and shout it each time they hit a triangle. The group goal is 101 combined. Your job is to roam the grid and knock balls away — go after your stronger players first.
What to Look For
Players using small touches to keep the ball close when you approach. Heads up to spot both the triangles and you coming. The counting keeps players invested in the team total.
“How did you keep the ball safe when I came near you?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for outer grid and inner triangles
Activity 03
Wreck-It Ralph
Dribbling · Scanning · Shooting
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards with a cone goal in each corner, opening inward. Every player has a ball. Players dribble around the grid and score by dribbling their ball through any corner goal while you (Wreck-It Ralph) roam and try to knock balls away. Ball knocked out: 10 toe taps to re-enter. Play short rounds of about a minute. No small goals required, cone goals only.
What to Look For
Players who look up to find an open goal before driving toward it — that is scanning in action. Encourage players to beat their own score each round.
“Before you drove toward a goal, what did you check first?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for grid and corner goals
DRIBBLING & PROTECTING  —  Taking on defenders and keeping the ball safe
Activity 04
Duck Tails
Dribbling · Protecting · Competing
Setup
Grid about 20 by 20 yards. Every player has a ball and tucks a pinny in the back of their shorts. Players dribble around the space trying to steal a teammate's tail while protecting their own. Start with no elimination: if a tail falls off, tuck it back in and keep playing. For a final competitive round, switch to elimination — tail stolen means you are out. Last player with their tail wins. Count how many tails each player collects.
What to Look For
Players who face their opponent rather than just running away — that is the beginning of how they will take on a defender. Heads up to see where tails are and where opponents are coming from.
“What did you do when someone came to steal your tail?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for grid  ·  Pinnies as tails
Activity 05
Knockout
Dribbling · Protecting · Competing
Setup
Grid about 20 by 20 yards. Every player has a ball and dribbles freely. Players try to knock other players' balls out of the grid while keeping their own in. Ball knocked out: five toe taps outside the grid, then back in (non-elimination). For a final competitive round, switch to elimination — ball knocked out means you are out. Last player with their ball in the grid wins. Remind players to keep their ball close when an opponent approaches.
What to Look For
Players who shield the ball with their body rather than just running away. Players who look up to track both the ball and the incoming threat. This game naturally develops what Shield Steal teaches, in a more active and competitive format.
“When someone came to knock your ball out, what did you do to protect it?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for grid
Activity 06
Dinosaur Tag
Dribbling · Direction Changes
Setup
No outer boundary needed. Each pair sets up a triangle of three cones, with sides about 5 yards long. One player is the dinosaur, one is the park ranger. Both dribble around their triangle. The dinosaur tries to tag the park ranger. The park ranger can go any direction around the triangle but cannot leave the ball. Switch roles after every tag or after one minute. Hissing by the dinosaur is strongly encouraged.
What to Look For
Players learning to change direction quickly while keeping the ball close. Some players will struggle with the turns — that is fine. Encourage small touches with both inside and outside of the foot. The competition drives the skill.
“What did you do with the ball when you needed to change direction quickly?”
1 ball per pair  ·  3 cones per pair to form a triangle
SCANNING  —  Looking up: the foundational awareness skill
Activity 07
Gates Dribbling
Driving · Scanning
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards with 8 to 10 gates scattered throughout. Each gate is two tall cones placed an arm's length apart. Every player has a ball. Score a point by dribbling through any gate. 60-second challenge: how many gates can you get through? Tell players to imagine a force field the size of their arms — the ball should stay inside it while they dribble. Progress: call a specific cone color and players must look up to find those gates first.
What to Look For
Players who keep their head up find more gates. The game creates the need for scanning without you naming it. After the question, name it: “That looking up while you dribble — that is called scanning.”
“How did you find the gates faster? What were you doing with your eyes?”
1 ball per player  ·  8 to 10 tall cones in pairs  ·  Disc cones for grid  ·  Timer
Activity 08
Shadow Dribbling
Scanning · Dribbling
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards. Players in pairs, each with their own ball. One player is the leader who dribbles however they want: fast, slow, turning, zigzagging. Their partner is the shadow, following close behind and mimicking every move. Switch roles every minute. If a leader tries something fancy, encourage the shadow to try it too even if it is not an exact copy.
What to Look For
The shadow must have their head up to follow the leader. Players who look at their own ball fall behind. The game builds the reflex to look up naturally, without you ever asking for it.
“How did you stay close to your leader? What did you have to keep your eyes on?”
1 ball per player  ·  Disc cones for grid
Activity 09
Color Tag Dribble
Scanning · Driving
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards with colored tall cones placed around the space. Every player has a ball and dribbles freely. Hold up a colored cone — all players must dribble to touch a cone of that color before you count to ten. Vary the colors and the pace. Progress: have two callers with different colors at the same time so players must split their attention.
What to Look For
Players who keep their head up while dribbling reach the color fastest. Let the game teach the lesson. After the question, you can name it: “That is scanning.”
“Who got to the cone fastest? What were you doing with your head while you were dribbling?”
1 ball per player  ·  Multiple colored tall cones  ·  Disc cones for grid
ATTACKING PLAY  —  Getting to goal, beating defenders, scoring
Activity 10
Shooting Gallery
Shooting · Decision-Making
Setup
Grid about 20 by 20 yards with a cone goal on the center of each side, all opening inward — four goals total. Every player has their own ball. Players dribble freely inside the grid and shoot at any goal they choose. After the shot, retrieve the ball and go again. No groups, no queues, everyone active the whole time. Challenge: how many different goals can you score on in 60 seconds?
What to Look For
Players who look up to find an open goal before driving toward it. Volume of attempts is the point — confidence to shoot develops now and accuracy comes with time. Celebrate every goal. A player who scores on all four goals in one round deserves a big reaction.
“Before you shot, what did you look at?”
1 ball per player  ·  8 tall cones for 4 goals (pairs, arm's length apart)  ·  Disc cones for grid  ·  Timer
Activity 11
1v1 to Endlines & Goals
Dribbling · Attacking · Defending
Setup
Pairs working in rectangles about 10 by 15 yards. 1v1 to Endlines: players score by dribbling the ball over their opponent's end line with the ball within arm's reach of their body. No shooting — more touches and more decisions. Progress to 1v1 to Cone Goals: add a cone goal at each end and players score by kicking through below knee height. Run all pairs simultaneously. Fast rotations keep everyone active.
What to Look For
Players trying to dribble past the defender rather than just kicking and chasing. Reward the attempt regardless of outcome. If players are unevenly matched, play the ball directly to the less skilled player to level the challenge.
“When you had the ball, did you try to go past your opponent or around them? What worked?”
1 ball per pair  ·  4 disc cones per rectangle  ·  Tall cones for goals (optional)  ·  Pinnies
Activity 12
2v1 to Goal
Shooting · Passing · Decision-Making
Setup
Groups of 3. Two attackers against one defender, attacking a cone goal about 6 yards wide. Ball starts with one attacker around 10 yards out. Rotate roles every 30 seconds. Run 2 to 3 groups simultaneously depending on how many adults are available to manage them. Do not coach the pass — let players discover the advantage of having a teammate free. Rotate defenders regularly so they do not get discouraged.
What to Look For
Do not coach the combination. If a player passes to a free teammate and they score, celebrate it loudly and specifically: “Did everyone see that? Two players just solved a problem that one player couldn't.” That moment is the first seed of ‘Me & My Teammates.’
“When you had two players against one defender, what options did you have? Did you use both of them?”
1 ball per group  ·  2 tall cones per goal  ·  Pinnies  ·  Run 2 to 3 groups simultaneously
Closing Play
5v5 — Back to the Game
Same format as the opening. Let it run. Step all the way back.

Standard Format

Same 5v5 game as the opening. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. Quick restarts after every goal. The space can be slightly larger than the opening game to give players more room. Let it run without stopping.

Alternative Format

Use one large goal at one end with two cone goals (or puggs) at the halfway line. One team defends the large goal, the other scores through the halfway cones. Gives players a taste of a more directional game and creates a natural attacking and defending dynamic. Either format works.
Closing Question — Ask it at the end of the session “What is one thing you did today that you want to try again next week?”
⚽ 1 ball per team 🔴 Mini goals, cone goals, or 1 large goal + puggs 🟠 Disc cones for boundaries 🟡 Pinnies — 2 colors
Coaching Cues
What to Watch For and How to Respond
Player looks up while dribbling

“Yes — that is scanning. You are already looking for what is next. That is a big deal at any age.”

Player tries to dribble past and fails

“I love that you tried it. What would you do differently next time?”

Player passes and teammate scores

Celebrate it loudly. Do not over-explain why it worked — let the result speak for itself.

Player stands still without the ball

Do not instruct. Ask: “Where could you move to help your team right now?”

Player gets frustrated after a mistake

“Every player in the world loses the ball. The best ones go and win it straight back.”

Session feels chaotic

That is okay. Controlled chaos at U8 is normal. If everyone has a ball and is moving, you are doing it right.

Season Plan
8-Week Suggested Rotation

The opening and closing game stays the same every week. The practice activity rotates. The first four weeks lean on driving, ball skills, and scanning — the core of ‘Me & the Ball.’ The second four weeks introduce more competitive and attacking contexts. Week 8 is yours.

Week Focus Activity What to Watch For
1 Driving the ball Red Light Green Light Players keeping the ball close at yellow; heads up at green
2 Dribbling under pressure 101 Dalmatians Players using small touches when you get close; players counting out loud
3 Scanning — looking up Gates Dribbling Players who find more gates because their heads are up
4 Scanning in pairs Shadow Dribbling Shadows who stay close because they are watching the leader, not their own ball
5 Direction changes and protecting Dinosaur Tag Players using both feet to turn; the competitive rounds in Duck Tails can also run here
6 Competing for the ball Knockout Players shielding with their body; players keeping heads up to track threats
7 Shooting and attacking Shooting Gallery or 2v1 to Goal Players looking up before shooting; any moment of combination play in the 2v1
8 Coach's choice — use what your group loves most or needs most. Both are correct answers.
Session Setup
What You'll Need Each Session
Balls — 1 per player (Size 3)The most important resource. With 12 players you need 12 balls. Without a ball per player, the practice activities in this guide do not work as designed.
Disc Cones — 20 per teamFor grids, boundaries, and cone triangles in Dinosaur Tag. Flat disc cones are safer than tall cones at this age.
Tall Cones — 8 to 10 per teamUsed as cone goals (pairs, arm's length apart) and as gates in dribbling activities. Multiple colors support directional cues like “dribble to the red cone.”
Pinnies — Full squad, 2 colorsEnough for the whole group in two distinct colors so teams are clear during games.
TimerTimed challenges create urgency and competition naturally. A phone works perfectly.
Mini goals or puggs (optional)Useful for the opening and closing 5v5 game when available. Cone goals work as a fallback at any point.
Player Development
Where Your Players Will Be

These are the skills we hope to see developing by the end of the season. All players develop at different rates and player development is not linear — approach this with a flexible mindset.

Moving on to U10

Keeps a moving ball close while running
Changes direction without losing control
Looks up sometimes while dribbling — the habit is forming
Receives a short pass and does something intentional with it
Tries to beat defenders rather than just kicking away
Notices teammates and occasionally tries to involve them
