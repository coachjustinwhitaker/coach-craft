Coach Craft  |  Player Development

U10 Coach Companion Me & the Ball  ·  Me & My Teammates  ·  Me & My Team Defending

Foundation Phase II  ·  7v7 Format  ·  2 Practices Per Week  ·  Recreational
Session Length:70–75 minutes total  ·  Arrive 15 minutes early

The game gets bigger this year. The field is wider, there are more players, and the problems are more complex. The good news is your players are ready for it. They are starting to discover what every great player knows: figuring it out with a teammate is more satisfying than figuring it out alone.

US Soccer Alignment
Foundation Phase II  ·  7v7 Format

Every activity in this guide reflects what US Soccer recommends for this age group. The 7v7 format gives players enough space and enough teammates to start experiencing those moments in a real game.

Who Is In Front of You
Understanding Your U10 Players

How They Have Grown

They have more sustained attention and can begin to understand that their actions affect teammates. They are ready to solve problems together, not just individually.

What Motivates Them

Scoring, competing, and belonging. The social side of the game intensifies at this age. They care about their teammates. Celebrate assists as loudly as goals.

The Big Shift

Passing is no longer just an option. It is becoming a tool. Players are beginning to understand that combining with a teammate can beat a defender. That insight is what this year is built around.

What They Are Ready For

2v1 situations, moving after passing, body shape to receive, and basic combination play. Set plays and formations come later. Right now, the game itself is the best teacher.

What a Great U10 Session Looks Like
Players move after passing

The give-and-go instinct is forming. Any session where players pass and then stand still is a session where that habit is not developing.

Body shape opens to the field

Players receiving with their hips at 45–90 degrees toward goal. Name it when you see it. This is a skill that takes years to become automatic.

2v1 situations recognized

Players starting to sense when they have a teammate free. They don’t always use it yet. The recognition is the first step.

Players talking to each other

Even one player calling for the ball is a win. Communication is a skill and it starts with the simplest signal.

High energy across both practices

Two practices a week means twice the opportunity to build habits. Keep both sessions varied and active. The second practice builds on the first.

Coach with intention

Pick one thing to look for before the session starts. Watch for it. When it happens, ask one good question. That is more effective than a dozen instructions from the sideline.

Teaching Points for This Age Group
What You Are Developing
ControllingProtectingDriving DribblingPassingShooting ScanningSupportingBody Shape CoveringMarking

Two perceptual skills get significantly more emphasis this year. Supporting now means finding open space AND moving after giving a pass: repositioning after the pass is the give-and-go in its simplest form. Body Shape means shoulders and hips at 45–90 degrees toward the attacking goal when receiving, so the player can already see the field when the ball arrives. These two actions together are the foundation of combination play. Defending joins the plan this year too. Covering means standing behind the pressing teammate at a distance that lets you step in if they are beaten. Marking means staying close to an opponent and between that player and the goal. Together they are how a group of kids starts to defend as a team.

Before You Arrive
What Prepared Looks Like
The best sessions start before the first player arrives. Mark the fields, set up the goals, and know your two activities before anyone shows up. Practice A introduces the concept for the week. Practice B deepens it. Know which one you are running before you get there.
The Parents on the Sideline

Before the session starts, take 30 seconds to walk over and say something like:

“Today we are working on passing and moving: making a pass and then finding space to get it back. The most helpful thing you can do is cheer when they pass to a teammate, especially if they then keep running.”

Most parents want to know what is happening and how to help. Give them a role and they will take it.

Opening Play
4v4 or 5v5 — Free Small-Sided Game
Same setup every session, all season. Players should know this game before they arrive.

The Setup

Run one or two small fields simultaneously: 3v3 or 4v4. Small fields mean more touches and nowhere to hide. Mini goals or cone goals at each end. No goalkeeper, no offside. Ball out: kick-in or dribble-in from the sideline. Quick restarts after every goal. Same format every session so players arrive already knowing the game.

Your Job

Come in with your Activity A already chosen based on what you want to work on this week. The opening game confirms your read and shows you how ready the group is. On Practice B days, watch specifically for whether anything from Practice A earlier in the week shows up without being asked for.
One Question — Ask it after the opening game “When you received the ball, where was your first touch? Did it help you go forward or did it take you sideways?”
⚽ 1 ball per team🏈 2 small goals🟠 Disc cones🟡 Pinnies — 2 colors
Practice
Activity Toolkit — Pick Two Per Session

Run Activity A for 15 minutes, then give players a brief water break or transition before Activity B. That reset gives them a chance to catch their breath and gives you a moment to set up the next game. Activity A is the anchor: familiar, physical, high-repetition. Activity B is where the concept for the week lives.

The Pairing Rule Try to avoid pairing two new activities in the same session. Changing grids gives players a mental break, so mixing up the physical setup between Activity A and B helps too. The best pairs: one that gets hearts pumping, one that requires them to think.
ME & THE BALL  —  Individual skill, pressure, scanning, body shape
Activity 01
Flying Numbers
Decision-Making · Variable Pressure
Setup
Mark space with cones, small goals on both sides. Two teams along the touchline either side of you. Call a number combo (1v1, 2v1, 2v2, 3v2) and each side sends in that number to scrimmage. New players rotate in after every goal or ball out. Call different numbers from each side to create imbalanced situations.
What to Look For
The most adaptable game in the toolkit. If one player is dominating, play them 1v2. If a quieter player needs more touches, play the ball directly to them. You control the challenge level in real time. Works perfectly as an anchor activity because it never feels the same twice.
“When you had more players than the other team, what did you do with that extra person?”
→ Pairs well with: Shrinking Field, 4v2 Keep-Away, or 1v1 to Goals
1 ball · 2 goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 02
Shrinking Field
First Touch · Body Shape · Pressure
Setup
Rectangular grid with goals at each end. 3v3 or 4v4. Every minute, move each touchline in by 3 steps. The field narrows progressively. Players must make decisions faster as space disappears. Team with most goals after 3 minutes wins the round. Play 2–3 rounds.
What to Look For
Players whose first touch opens their body, hips at 45 to 90 degrees toward goal, have more time in tight spaces. Players who receive square-on get pressed immediately. On the second visit, frame it as a defending focus: which team stays compact and presses together as the field shrinks?
“As the field got smaller, what changed about when you decided to pass?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or 4v2 Keep-Away
1 ball · 2 goals · Disc cones
Activity 03
1v1 to Endlines & Goals
Dribbling · Defending · Control
Setup
1v1 to Endlines: small rectangle, score by dribbling ball over opponent’s end line within arm’s distance. Progress to 1v1 to Small Goals: add cone goals at each end, score below knee height. Pairs run simultaneously. Fast rotations. Keep multiple pairs active at once.
What to Look For
At U10 the focus shifts: not just beating the defender, but the decision: dribble or shoot? When is each the right answer? Players who look up to check for the goal before driving are already thinking ahead. Works well as an anchor because every player is active simultaneously.
“When you got past your opponent, what made you decide to shoot rather than keep dribbling?”
→ Pairs well with: Gates Passing or 2v2 to Endlines
1 ball per pair · 4 cones per game · Pinnies · Small goals optional
Activity 04
Rondo
Possession · Body Shape · Quick Passing
Setup
Square grid, one defender in the center, four attackers on the outside. This is called a rondo. Attackers keep the ball away from the defender. When the defender wins it or the ball goes out, the attacker who last touched it swaps into the middle. Keep track of consecutive passes. Progress: add a second defender (4v2) if passing is excellent, or drop to 3v1 to force attackers to move more.
What to Look For
Look for attackers spreading out to give the player with the ball multiple options. Players who bunch together make the defender's job easy. The player with the ball should always have at least two teammates they can see. On the receiving end, open hips give a player more time before the defender closes in.
“When you were in the middle defending, what made it harder to win the ball? What made it easier?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Gates Passing
1 ball per group · 4 cones per square · Pinnies for defenders
ME & MY TEAMMATES  —  Passing, combination play, creating and using space
Activity 05
Gates Passing
Passing · Move After Passing · Communication
Setup
Rectangular grid with 8–10 gates scattered throughout (two cones arm’s length apart). Players in pairs with one ball. Score by passing to each other through gates. After passing, move immediately to a new gate. Encourage players to tell their partner where they are going before they pass.
What to Look For
The pass-and-move habit is the entire point. A player who passes and stands still is not learning “Me & My Teammates.” Look for any player who moves after passing. Name it when you see it. Also look for open body shape on receiving. Progress: replace the gates with cone triangles scattered around the grid. Players score by passing through a triangle to their partner. Now add a challenge: tell your partner where you are going before you pass. The pair that communicates most will score the most.
“After you passed the ball, what did you do next? Why does moving matter?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Rondo
1 ball per pair · 8–10 tall cone gates · Disc cones for grid
Activity 06
End Zone Passing
Supporting Runs · Move Off the Ball · Combination Play
Setup
Rectangular grid with a small end zone marked at each end (about 4 yards deep). Two teams of 3 or 4. Players can dribble freely in the middle of the field, but the only way to score is to pass the ball to a teammate who has run into the opponent's end zone to receive it. The receiver must have both feet inside the end zone when they catch the ball.
What to Look For
This game makes movement off the ball essential. A team that stands still cannot score because no one is in the end zone to receive. Look for players who time their run to arrive as the ball is being played. Any player who calls for the ball while running into the end zone is showing real game intelligence. Celebrate those moments explicitly.
“When did you run into the end zone? What were you watching to know when to go?”
→ Pairs well with: Gates Passing or Flying Numbers
1 ball · Disc cones for grid and end zones · Pinnies
Activity 07
Numbers Passing
Passing Sequence · Body Shape · Anticipation
Setup
Grid with 4 cones. Assign each player a number, their passing order. Players jog around the space passing in numbered order: 1 to 2, 2 to 3, until the last number passes back to 1. Add a second ball when they find a rhythm. Optional: add a goal. The last player shoots, then becomes the new number 1.
What to Look For
Look for players keeping hips open (not square-on), cushioning the ball softly, then taking a purposeful next touch. Players who are always looking for the next number before the ball arrives will stand out. That anticipation is body shape doing its job. Works well as a focus activity after Gates Passing.
“How did you know where the next number was before the ball came to you?”
→ Pairs well with: 1v1 to Endlines or Flying Numbers
1 ball (2 when ready) · Disc cones for grid
Activity 08
2v2 to Endlines — Attacking
Spreading Out · 2v1 Situations
Setup
Small rectangular space. Two attackers vs two defenders. Attackers score by dribbling ball over opponent’s end line within arm’s distance. Defenders can steal and score too. Rotate new players when scoring or ball out. Keep it fast. Run multiple games simultaneously.
What to Look For
Encourage the attacker with the ball to drive at one defender to commit them, while the second attacker finds open space. When they spread out and use that advantage, celebrate it. The moment a player passes around a committed defender, even once, is worth highlighting for the whole group.
“When you had the ball and a defender coming at you, what options did you have?”
→ Pairs well with: 1v1 to Goals or Flying Numbers
1 ball per game · 4 cones per game · Pinnies
Activity 09
3v2 to Goal
Numerical Advantage · Combination Play
Setup
Rectangular grid, small goal at one end. Three attackers from one end meet two defenders. Attackers work together to score. Defenders can score by dribbling over the end line. Remind attackers to spread out. Rotate defenders so they don’t get discouraged.
What to Look For
Do not explain the combination in advance. Run the game and watch it emerge. If a player dribbles at a defender to force commitment and then passes around them, freeze the moment briefly: “Did everyone see that? What happened there?” That is the whole concept in one moment.
“When did you decide to pass rather than keep dribbling? What did you see that told you to pass?”
→ Pairs well with: 2v2 to Endlines or Flying Numbers
1 ball · 1 small goal · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 10
Five Goal Game
Finding New Paths · Problem Solving
Setup
Rectangular grid with a cone goal in each corner plus one in the center, 5 goals total. 3v3 or 4v4. Players try to pass the ball through any goal to a teammate to score in any direction. Defenders protect all 5 goals and can only gain possession by intercepting or stealing.
What to Look For
Attackers must adapt quickly when their path is blocked. Players without the ball must move to open space near goals so their teammate has someone to find. Best as a focus activity later in the season when combination play is starting to click.
“When the goal you were aiming for was blocked, what did you do?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or 3v2 to Goal
1 ball · 5 goal pairs (4 corners + 1 center) · Pinnies
Activity 11
Barcelona
Patience · Pass Counting · Combination Play
Setup
Rectangular grid with goals at each end. 3v3 or 4v4, no goalkeeper. Score points equal to the number of passes completed before scoring: 8 passes before a goal = 8 points. A missed shot scores nothing. Play short rounds of 3–4 minutes.
What to Look For
Teams that learn to move the ball patiently before shooting will outscore teams that rush. Watch for players who switch the ball to the far side before going to goal. That is combination play at its most sophisticated. Best as a focus activity late in the season when passing habits are forming.
“Before you scored, was there a moment where you switched the ball to the other side? What did that open up?”
→ Pairs well with: Rondo 5v2 or Five Goal Game
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones · Pinnies
Activity 12
Rondo 5v2
Quick Passing · Body Shape · Controlled Touches
Setup
Square of cones. Five attackers in a circle, two defenders in the center. Attackers keep the ball away. Track consecutive passes. Attacker who last touched an interception or ball out of bounds swaps with a defender. Rotate defenders every few minutes. Limit to 1–2 touches for extra challenge.
What to Look For
Where the Rondo (4v1) is a small tight game, this is the bigger version: more players, two defenders, faster decisions required. Quick touches and spreading the space are the two things to reinforce. Encourage attackers to draw a defender toward them before passing. The moment a defender commits, there is a free player somewhere. That read is the whole game.
“How did you draw the defender in before passing? What would have happened if you passed straight away?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Shrinking Field
1 ball · 4 cones for square · Pinnies for defenders
ME & MY TEAM DEFENDING  —  Pressing, delaying, covering, marking, staying compact
Activity 13
Approach & Delay
Pressing · Delaying · Challenging
Setup
Rectangular grid about 10 by 15 yards. An attacker with the ball starts at one end line, a defender starts 5 yards away. The attacker scores by dribbling over the far end line. The defender wins by taking the ball or knocking it out. Rotate after every turn. Teach one picture: run at the ball quickly, then slow down as you arrive, with the body turned a little to the side.
What to Look For
Fast approach, slow arrival. A defender who lunges and gets dribbled past has learned something too. Name the player who slows down and stays on their feet, even if the attacker gets by. Delaying is a win at this age. Progress: add a second defender who starts behind the first and calls “cover.”
“When you got close to the dribbler, what did you do with your feet and your speed?”
→ Pairs well with: Flying Numbers or Shrinking Field
1 ball per group · Disc cones for the grid · Pinnies
Activity 14
Pressure & Cover 3v3
Cover · Recover · Working Together
Setup
Grid about 25 by 20 yards with three cone gates on one end line. Attackers (3) score by passing or dribbling through any gate. Defenders (3) win the ball and play it out across the opposite end line. Before each round, defenders name who is first (presses), who is second (covers), and who is third. Freeze the game once or twice per round and ask each defender where they are standing.
What to Look For
The second defender stands behind and to the side of the first, close enough to step in if they are beaten. Look for the defender who steps in after a teammate is dribbled past instead of watching. Look for players who talk: “I have cover.” Do not fix everything at once. Pick one picture per round.
“When your teammate pressed, where did you stand? What did you do when they got beaten?”
→ Pairs well with: Approach & Delay or Flying Numbers
1 ball · 3 gates (6 tall cones) · Disc cones for the grid · Pinnies
Activity 15
Shadow Marking
Marking · Goal Side · Interceptions
Setup
Grid about 30 by 20 yards with a small goal at each end, no goalkeepers. 4v4. Give each defender one attacker to stay with (match pinnie colors or numbers). A goal counts double if the scoring player was marked closely, and an interception counts as a point. Everyone plays defender and attacker. Play two minutes per round, then swap who marks whom.
What to Look For
Marking means staying between your player and the goal, close enough to step in when the ball is played. Look for defenders who keep one eye on the ball and one on their player. A defender who ball-watches loses their player. Celebrate the player who steps in front and steals the pass.
“When your player moved, how did you decide how close to stay?”
→ Pairs well with: Pressure & Cover 3v3 or Flying Numbers
1 ball · 2 small goals · Disc cones · Pinnies in 2 colors, numbered if possible
Activity 16
Squeeze the Space
Compact Shape · Narrow in the Middle · Deny the Finish
Setup
Half field with a goal and a goalkeeper (or a large cone goal). Mark two wide channels, about 5 yards wide, along the sidelines. 5 attackers v 4 defenders. Attackers score from the middle or by crossing from a wide channel. Defenders start by getting narrow and close to the center, then shift toward the ball. Call “squeeze” and the defenders tighten up around the ball.
What to Look For
Defenders closer together than they were a minute ago. A shape that shifts as the ball moves instead of chasing it. Watch for the wide defender who keeps the cross from being easy, and the players who block the shot instead of turning away. Winning the ball is a bonus. Making the shot hard is the goal.
“When the ball went wide, where did the rest of your team go?”
→ Pairs well with: Approach & Delay or Shadow Marking
1 ball · 1 goal and keeper · Disc cones for channels · Pinnies
Season Plan
Sample Rotation  ·  Two Practices Per Week

Each week has two sessions. Activity A anchors the session with something physical and largely familiar. Activity B introduces or deepens the concept for that week. The first two weeks focus on “Me & the Ball” under pressure in the new 7v7 environment. Weeks three through five shift the focus to “Me & My Teammates.” Weeks six and seven add “Me & My Team Defending.” Week eight is yours.

Closing Play
5v5 or 7v7 — Back to the Game
Bigger than the opening game. Game-realistic. 22 minutes. No stoppages.

The Setup

5v5 or full 7v7 if numbers allow. Proper goals or large cone goals. No offside to start. As players grow through the season and games start to look more organized, you can introduce offside in simple terms. For now, let them play freely and focus on putting their new skills to work. Quick restarts after every goal.

What to Notice

Did anything from Activity A or B show up? A player who opened their body to receive. A pass-and-move that created a goal. A 2v1 recognized and used. You do not need to name it. Noticing it is evidence the session worked. On Practice B days, check whether anything carried over from Practice A earlier in the week?
Closing Question “What is one moment today where a pass to a teammate made something happen that you couldn’t have done alone?”
Coaching Cues
What to Watch For and How to Respond
Player moves after passing

“Yes, that is exactly it. Now your teammate has an option they didn’t have before you moved.”

Player opens hips when receiving

“Did you see that? They already knew where they were going before the ball arrived. That is body shape.”

Player finds teammate in 2v1

Celebrate it clearly: “That is combination play. Two players just solved a problem that one couldn’t.”

Players bunching around the ball

Ask: “Where is the space on this field right now? Who could go find it?”

Activity A finishes flat

That is the cue to shorten Activity B or swap it for something more physical. Two activities only works if the first one actually runs well.

Concept from Practice A shows up in Practice B

Name it explicitly: “You did that earlier this week. Your body remembered.” That connection is exactly what two practices per week is for.

Player Development
Where Your Players Will Be

These are the skills we hope to see developing by the end of the season. All players develop at different rates and not every player will show all of these. That is completely normal.

Moving on to U12

Keeps the ball under pressure rather than kicking it away
Looks up before receiving, not just after
Passes with a reason, not just to get rid of the ball
Moves to a new spot after passing, at least sometimes
Recognizes when a teammate is free and tries to find them
Pressures the ball or covers a teammate, at least sometimes
Competes in 7v7 with confidence and enjoyment
Shows up wanting to play again
